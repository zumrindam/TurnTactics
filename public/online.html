    Chat privacy, limits and retention

    Direct messages are visible only to their two participants inside the app. They are not end-to-end encrypted: database administrators can access stored messages. The host cannot read other people’s direct messages and cannot mute anyone. Chat is not the official record — only Dispatch confirms that an action happened. Text only; 1000 characters; one message per second and 20 per minute. Messages cannot be edited or withdrawn. When a game finishes, its chat is deleted 24 hours later (sooner if the host deletes the finished room). Anything already seen, screenshotted or copied elsewhere cannot be erased.

    How the game works

    The host is a referee, not a player. Every player starts with three hearts on a unique random square. The field opens at the next Pacific midnight; each living player then receives income at every midnight. Unspent points accumulate.

    Range is Manhattan distance (squares across plus squares up or down), so diagonal and L-shaped targets count when they are within range. Range is for attacks and transfers, not for movement.

    Confirming an action is your declaration. Only a server-accepted action changes the game; a rejected or interrupted request costs nothing. Attacks, transfers, shields and other players’ resources are private. Movement, eliminations and rule changes are public in Dispatch.

    Eliminated players keep chat and may vote for a living player; a strict majority of ballots cast awards a bonus at midnight. Ballots are private.

    The referee sees every resource and a private action log, and must not reveal them.

    Rule history

    Every approved change, newest first. “Host-enforced” rules are written agreements the referee upholds; the game engine does not apply them.

      Rules desk

      Build an amendment from typed effects, preview exactly what it does, then publish it. Effects in one amendment apply together or not at all. Anything the catalog cannot express goes in as a written rule, which is labelled host-enforced.

        What the rules desk will not do

        It never backdates income, replays past events, revives players, moves pieces or resizes an occupied board. It cannot open anyone’s messages, change sign-in or recovery protections, mute players, remove chat from eliminated players, reveal private resources, or run code. Published amendments are permanent public history: a correction is a new amendment, not an erasure.
